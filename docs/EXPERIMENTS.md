## 2. Parameter Logs & Baseline Details

All models are trained with a fixed random seed of `42`.

*   **Logistic Regression:** `C=0.1`, L2 penalty, `solver='lbfgs'`, `max_iter=1000`. Standardized scaling applied only to the continuous portion of features (Descriptors and continuous portion of Combined) fit on training data.
*   **Random Forest:** `n_estimators=250`, `max_depth=12`, `min_samples_split=5`, `min_samples_leaf=2`, `max_features='sqrt'`, `class_weight='balanced'`. No feature scaling applied.
*   **XGBoost:** `n_estimators=200`, `max_depth=6`, `learning_rate=0.05`, `subsample=0.8`, `colsample_bytree=0.8`, `min_child_weight=2`. No class weight parameters applied. No feature scaling.

## 3. Generalization Gap Observations & Analysis

### A. Random Split (In-Distribution) Performance
*   *Performance:* Models trained on the Random Stratified split perform exceptionally well, achieving accuracies of 86-87% and ROC-AUCs of 0.92-0.96.
*   *Highlights:* **Random Forest on Descriptors** achieved the highest accuracy (**87.36%**) and Macro F1 (**0.8604**), while **Logistic Regression on Combined Features** achieved the highest ROC-AUC (**0.9466**) closely followed by **XGBoost on Combined** (**0.9458**).
*   *Insights:* Physicochemical descriptors alone provide a very strong signal. Adding Morgan fingerprints yields marginal performance gains on in-distribution data, but significantly increases feature space dimensionality (from 11 to 2,059).

### B. Scaffold Split (Out-of-Distribution) Performance
*   *Performance:* When evaluated on structurally novel scaffolds (scaffold split), all models show a distinct drop in performance.
*   *Highlights:* **Random Forest on Descriptors** remains the top-performing model in raw accuracy (**74.47%**) and Macro F1 (**0.7212**), showing strong structural generalization.
*   *Fingerprint Collapse:* Models using **Fingerprints Only** on the scaffold split perform poorly (accuracy drops to 66-69% and Macro F1 drops to 0.55-0.63). This is because structural bits seen in training are not present in novel scaffolds, causing sparse representation models to fail.
*   *Combined Synergy:* Combining fingerprints and descriptors creates the highest ranking power under out-of-distribution conditions. **XGBoost on Combined features** achieved the overall highest ROC-AUC (**0.8138**) and PR-AUC (**0.8684**).

### C. Generalization Gap (Macro F1 Drop)
The "generalization gap" is computed as the difference in Macro F1-Score between Random and Scaffold evaluations:

*   *Random Forest (Descriptors):* 0.8604 (Random) $\rightarrow$ 0.7212 (Scaffold) $\rightarrow$ **-0.1392** drop.
*   *XGBoost (Combined):* 0.8568 (Random) $\rightarrow$ 0.6838 (Scaffold) $\rightarrow$ **-0.1730** drop.
*   *Logistic Regression (Fingerprints):* 0.8488 (Random) $\rightarrow$ 0.6316 (Scaffold) $\rightarrow$ **-0.2172** drop.
*   *Takeaway:* Fingerprints-only models are highly vulnerable to scaffold shifts, showing the largest generalization gap (-0.2172). Physicochemical descriptors are the most robust to structural distribution shifts (-0.1392 drop), as size, lipophilicity, and charge general rules hold true regardless of the ring scaffold shape.

## 4. Preliminary Feature Importance ranking (Descriptors)

Based on descriptor-only models:
1.  **TPSA (Topological Polar Surface Area):** Highest importance in Random Forest (0.283) and high in XGBoost (0.181). High polar surface area strongly impedes blood-brain barrier crossing.
2.  **H-Bond Donors:** Ranked most important in XGBoost (0.190) and second in Random Forest (0.115). More hydrogen donors make a molecule hydrophilic and block passive diffusion.
3.  **Formal Charge:** Third most important in XGBoost (0.181). Highly charged molecules do not passively cross lipid membranes.
4.  **LogP (Lipophilicity):** Third most important in Random Forest (0.114) and moderate in XGBoost. Sufficient lipophilicity is required to partition into the lipid bilayer.
